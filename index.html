BEND OFFSET

Bend Offset

Matching Bends

Two equal-and-opposite bends (same angle, mirrored) connected by a straight piece of pipe — the pipe exits parallel to how it entered, just shifted up or down. This is the verified case. A version for two different bend angles isn't built yet — that math still needs to be derived and checked, so it's intentionally left out rather than guessed at.

Center-to-end (C-E) is the measurement from the center of the fitting to the internal stop shoulder (where the pipe is seated inside the bell). Leave blank for a vertex-to-vertex distance instead of pipe length calculation.

Flush / Flow Rate

Discharge Flow

Flow rate = 29.83 × C × d² × √P — where d is the diameter of the actual discharge opening (valve, nozzle, corp stop), not the pipe's own diameter. C values below are the standard NFPA/IFSTA fire-flow coefficients — check the shape of the opening to pick the right one.

MATERIAL VOLUME

Material Volume

Shape

For an odd-shaped hole, wedge, or triangular patch or cross-section. Base can be any one side of the triangle — height is just the perpendicular distance from that side to the opposite corner, whichever way the shape sits.

For a straight pipe or cylindrical volume — e.g. the pipe itself.

Concrete or slurry encasement — encasement volume minus the volume the pipe(s) displace. Use the pipe's actual outside diameter (OD), not invert or ID.

CONFINED SPACE VENT.

Confined Space Ventilation

Not an OSHA/Cal-OSHA compliance number Neither OSHA nor Cal/OSHA sets a minimum CFM tied to space size — both require whatever forced-air ventilation it takes to pass atmospheric testing (O₂, LEL, PELs), continuously, for as long as anyone is inside. This tool gives a rule-of-thumb blower size using the common 20 air-changes-per-hour industry guidance (ANSI Z117.1), nothing more. It does not replace atmospheric testing, a confined space entry permit, or your company's safety program — always confirm entry conditions with a calibrated gas meter, not this number.

REFERENCE

Rod Units & Grade Math

Rod Types
Lenker Rod
Reads true elevation directly. No HI needed. Simplest rod, most direct number.
Engineer's Rod
Reading = HI − elevation. Decimal feet. Reading gets SMALLER as elevation gets HIGHER.
Architect's Rod
Same math as Engineer's Rod, displayed in feet-inches-1/16".
Elevation of the Instrument (HI)
Benchmark + Backsight
HI = benchmark elevation + backsight reading.
Calculate from Stakes
HI = stake elevation + rod reading shot at that stake. Works from any stake with both values — the app cross-checks multiple stakes automatically and flags disagreement over 0.05 ft.
Only needed for
Points entered as a typed Invert Elevation. A run made entirely of survey stakes never needs HI at all.
Survey Stake Math
True invert elevation
Cut: stake elevation − cut. Fill: stake elevation + fill. This is real elevation, for documentation — the surveyor's number, unrelated to your rod.
Rod reading at invert (Engineer's/Architect's Rod)
Cut: rod reading at stake + cut. Fill: rod reading at stake − fill. This is what you actually dial in — computed directly from your own shot, with no elevation math involved at all.
BOP / INV / TOP
BOP (Bottom of Pipe)
Elevation: INV − wall thickness. Engineer's/Architect's Rod reading: INV reading + wall thickness (reads HIGHER, not lower).
TOP (Top of Pipe)
Elevation: INV + pipe ID + wall thickness. Engineer's/Architect's Rod reading: INV reading − (ID + wall thickness) (reads LOWER, not higher).
Why BOP/TOP flip direction on Engineer's/Architect's Rod
Reading = HI − elevation. Since HI is fixed, a HIGHER elevation always gives a SMALLER reading. TOP is physically highest, so it reads lowest. BOP is physically lowest, so it reads highest.

REFERENCE

Pipe Cross-Section

Looking Straight Down the Pipe

This is a schematic, not to scale. TOP and BOP are the outside of the pipe (crown and bottom); INV is the inside bottom, where flow actually happens — the number that matters for grade. Springline is the horizontal centerline, the pipe's widest point.

Quick Reference
OD (Outside Diameter)
Full outer width of the pipe, wall included.
ID (Inside Diameter)
Open interior width — what "pipe size" (e.g. 12") almost always refers to. ID = OD − 2 × wall thickness.
Wall Thickness
The gap between OD and ID. (OD − ID) ÷ 2.
Springline
Horizontal line through the pipe's center — its widest point, and the standard reference line for utility conflict/clearance calls.
Circumference
π × OD — the full outer perimeter.

Slope & Grade

Slope
Slope %
(Start invert elevation − End invert elevation) ÷ Distance × 100.
Actual pipe length
√(horizontal distance² + fall²) — Pythagorean theorem. Slightly longer than the horizontal run distance on any sloped pipe.
Elevation change per interval
(Slope % ÷ 100) × interval distance. E.g. 2% slope over a 10 ft interval = 0.20 ft of fall (or rise) per interval — this is exactly how the app fills in the interval table between your points.
Percent / Ratio / Degrees
Percent → Ratio (1:X)
X = 100 ÷ percent.
Percent → Degrees
arctan(percent ÷ 100).
Ratio → Percent
(1 ÷ X) × 100.

Bends & Fittings

Bend Offset (symmetric — same angle both bends)
Vertex-to-vertex travel
Offset ÷ sin(angle).
Multipliers
11.25° → 5.126  ·  22.5° → 2.613  ·  45° → 1.414
Pipe cut length
Travel − C-E (fitting 1) − C-E (fitting 2).
Center-to-End (C-E)
Distance from a fitting's theoretical center to the internal stop shoulder — where the pipe actually seats inside the bell.
Asymmetric bends (different angle each end)
Not built into this calculator — math not yet verified.

Flow & Volume

Flush / Flow Rate
GPM
Q = 29.83 × C × d² × √P
C coefficients
Smooth/rounded 0.90  ·  Square/sharp 0.80  ·  Projecting into barrel 0.70  ·  Stream straightener 0.95
Material Volume
Rectangular
Length × Width × Depth
Triangular
0.5 × Base × Height × Depth
Cylindrical
π × r² × length
Encasement
(Encasement L × W × D) − sum of all pipe cylinder volumes inside it.
Manning's Equation (full-pipe gravity flow)
Velocity
(1.49 ÷ n) × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2), where R = diameter ÷ 4 (hydraulic radius, full pipe) and S = slope as a decimal.
Capacity
Velocity × cross-sectional area. This is the pipe's ceiling capacity flowing completely full — real day-to-day flow is usually partial-depth.

Pipe & Materials

Pipe Weight
Formula
π/4 × (OD² − ID²) × length × density. ID = OD − 2×wall thickness.
Approximate densities (lb/ft³)
Ductile Iron ~450  ·  PVC (any schedule/SDR/C900) ~90  ·  Solid-Wall HDPE ~59  ·  Steel ~490  ·  Concrete ~150
PVC schedule/SDR/C900
Same material density regardless of schedule — schedule changes wall thickness, not density. Enter your actual wall thickness for an accurate weight.
Corrugated HDPE
This formula assumes a solid, uniform wall. Corrugated pipe's ribbed profile has far less material than that — use the manufacturer's published weight for anything that matters.
Manning's n (roughness)
Typical values
PVC/HDPE 0.010  ·  Concrete/Vitrified Clay/Lined Ductile Iron 0.013  ·  Corrugated Metal 0.024
Self-cleansing velocity
~2 ft/s is the common minimum benchmark for a gravity sewer to avoid solids settling out.

Confined Space

Ventilation
CFM (rule of thumb, not an OSHA compliance number)
(Volume in ft³ × Air Changes per Hour) ÷ 60.
ACH tiers
20 = normal (ANSI Z117.1)  ·  40 = elevated hazard  ·  60 = extreme
Attendant Rule
OSHA 1910.146(i)
The attendant must stay outside the space at all times and may not enter for any reason — including rescue — unless first relieved by another qualified attendant.
Entry Conditions
Common reference range
O2 typically 19.5%–23.5%  ·  LEL typically below 10%. These are common references only — this app's automatic evacuation alarm only activates if you enter your own site-specific limits, since crews often rely on their sniffer's built-in alarm instead.
