# Glossary: English term → Spanish used in the app → why / alternatives (phases 1 + 2)

Audience: Spanish-speaking crew members on US underground jobs (mostly Mexican and Central American). Voice: **usted**, short
sentences, the words said on the job. Not Spain Spanish (no "vosotros", "ordenador", "pulsar", "añadir", "vale").

Three kinds of terms:

- **Kept in English** because that is what is written on the stake, the sheet, the pipe or the meter, or what the crew says.
- **Spanish** where there is one plain word everybody uses.
- **Review**: I picked one, a fluent person in the trade should confirm it. These are marked REVIEW.

Sources checked for the safety words: OSHA "Zanjas y excavaciones" fact sheet in Spanish
(osha.gov/OshDoc/data_Hurricane_Facts/trench_excavation_fs-sp.pdf), OSHA publication 4491 in Spanish
(osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA4491.pdf), OSHA confined space permit fact sheet in Spanish
(osha.gov/OshDoc/data_Hurricane_Facts/confined_space_permit_sp.pdf).

## Style rules used

1. **Usted** everywhere: "Toque", "Escriba", "Revise", "su foreman". Never "tú".
2. Buttons say what they do in 1 to 3 words: "Guardar contraseña", "Bloquear este tramo".
3. A button the person taps about themselves is first person: "Unirme a la cuadrilla", "Acepto".
4. Safety lines keep their capitals (NO ENTRE, EMERGENCIA) and their numbers and OSHA section numbers exactly.
5. Quotes from OSHA / NIOSH inside a message are my translation of the English rule text, marked in the strings file. They are
   not an official Spanish text of the rule.
6. No gendered guess about the reader where it can be avoided ("le mandó una invitación", not "lo invitó").
