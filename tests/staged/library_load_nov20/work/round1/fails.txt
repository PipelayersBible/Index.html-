   ANSWER (154 words): I can't call that readings-based go or no-go; the rule gives the numbers to test against, and the entry supervisor decides. California's construction rule (Title 8 Article 37, sections 1950 to 1962) mirrors federal Subpart AA. Oxygen below 19.5 percent is deficient and above 23.5 percent is enriched, and flammable gas above 10 percent of the LEL/LFL is a hazardous atmosphere [5].
- Those numbers only describe a hazardous atmosphere. They do not by themselves make a space safe to enter [5].
- Test first for oxygen, then combustible gases, then toxics (1926.1204(e)(3)). Continuous monitoring is required where entrants work, and sewers cannot be isolated [2][6]. An O2 and LEL reading alone doesn't cover H2S and CO.
- The permit must list the acceptable entry conditions and the test results, with the tester's name or initials and the time [4].

The entry supervisor decides on site; stop and ask them before anyone goes in.
