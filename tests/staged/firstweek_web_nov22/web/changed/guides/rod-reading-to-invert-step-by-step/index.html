Rod reading to invert, step by step

The height of instrument method, from the benchmark to the reading you lay pipe to.

By Thomas Fontaine, pipelayer and underground foreman. November 19, 2026.

The words

  • Benchmark (BM). A point with a known elevation, from the plans or the cut sheet. A hub with its elevation written on it works too.
  • Backsight (BS). The rod reading on the benchmark.
  • HI, height of instrument. The elevation of the line of sight of your level or laser.
  • Foresight (FS). Any rod reading after the backsight, on a point you want the elevation of.
  • Invert. The inside bottom of the pipe, where the water runs. Flowline means the same thing in the field.

Step 1: set up and shoot the benchmark

Set the instrument up where you can see the benchmark and the work, level it, and put the rod on the benchmark. Read it. That's your backsight.

Step 2: work out the HI

HI = benchmark elevation + backsight

The rod reading is how far the line of sight is above the benchmark, so adding it to the benchmark's elevation gives the elevation of the line of sight.

Example: the benchmark is 105.20 and the backsight is 4.36. The HI is 109.56.

Step 3: check the HI

Before you trust it, shoot a second known point, like another hub with its elevation on it. Work out the HI from that shot the same way. The two should agree within your tolerance. If they don't, find out why before you go on: a bumped instrument, a misread rod, a disturbed hub or a typo on the sheet. The instrument stays put once you have the HI. If it gets moved or bumped, start over at step 1.

Step 4: rod reading to elevation

elevation = HI − foresight

Put the rod on the point and read it. A reading of 13.53 with an HI of 109.56 is an elevation of 96.03.

Step 5: elevation to invert

Where the foot of the rod sits matters:

  • On the invert. The elevation is the invert.
  • On top of the pipe. Take off the outside diameter, then add back one wall: invert = elevation − OD + wall.
  • Under the pipe (bottom of pipe, BOP). Add one wall: invert = elevation + wall.

For 8" PVC SDR 35, the outside diameter is 8.4 inches and the minimum wall is 0.24 inches (8.4 ÷ 35). The top of the pipe sits 8.4 − 0.24 = 8.16 inches above the invert, which is 0.68 feet.

Step 6: the reading to lay to

Turn it around to set pipe. You know the design invert, so work out the rod reading that puts the pipe there:

reading to lay to = HI − elevation you want at the foot of the rod

Invert96.0313.53
Top of 8" SDR 3596.03 + 0.68 = 96.7112.85

All three use the HI of 109.56 and a design invert of 96.03. A higher reading means a lower point. If the rod on top of the pipe reads 12.88 when you want 12.85, the pipe is 0.03 feet low.

Grade down the line

The design invert changes as you go. At 0.40% it drops 0.10 feet every 25 feet, so the reading to lay to goes up 0.10 every 25 feet. Work out a row for each station before you start. The free pipe grade calculator gives the invert at every station.

Rod types

  • Engineer's rod. Feet, tenths and hundredths. All the math above works straight off it.
  • Architect's rod. Feet, inches and fractions. Convert to decimal feet first (inches ÷ 12), or do the whole thing in inches.
  • Lenker rod. A direct elevation rod. Once it's set on the benchmark, the number you read is the elevation, with no subtraction.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the foresight instead of subtracting it.
  • Forgetting which part of the pipe the rod was on.
  • Using the nominal size instead of the outside diameter for a top of pipe shot.
  • Not checking the HI on a second point.
  • Rod not plumb. A leaning rod reads high. Rock it slowly and take the lowest reading.
