Pipe volume and backfill

Gallons to fill a line for flushing or a test, and cubic yards of bedding and backfill for a trench with the pipe taken out.

Water in the pipe

Bedding and backfill

The formulas

pipe volume (ft³) = π × (diameter in inches ÷ 12 ÷ 2)² × length in feet

gallons = ft³ × 7.48052

trench (ft³) = width × depth × length

pipe zone (ft³) = width × (bedding + OD + cover) ÷ 12 × length − pipe volume (by OD)

backfill above (ft³) = width × (depth − pipe zone height) × length

cubic yards = ft³ ÷ 27

A US gallon is 231 cubic inches, and a cubic foot is 1,728, so there are 7.48052 gallons in a cubic foot. Water uses the inside diameter. Bedding and backfill take out the space the pipe fills, so they use the outside diameter.

Example with the numbers already filled in: 100 ft of 8" inside diameter holds 34.91 ft³, about 261 gallons (2.611 gallons a foot). A 3 ft wide, 6 ft deep, 100 ft trench is 66.67 yd³. 8" SDR 35 (8.4" outside) takes up 1.43 yd³ of it. With 6" of bedding and 12" of cover the pipe zone is 2.2 ft deep: 23.02 yd³ of pipe zone material and 42.22 yd³ of backfill above it.

These are in-place volumes for a trench with vertical walls. They don't add swell, compaction, waste, or the extra width of a sloped or benched cut, and inside diameters picked from the list are estimates. Use the pipe maker's numbers when they matter.
