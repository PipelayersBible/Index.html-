Rod reading to invert

The height of instrument method for an engineer's rod in decimal feet: shoot a benchmark, get the HI, then turn any rod reading into an elevation and an invert.

From the benchmark to the invert

Where the rod sits

How it works

HI = benchmark elevation + backsight

elevation = HI − rod reading

rod reading to bottom of pipe = HI − (design invert − wall)

With the rod on the invert, the elevation is the invert. On top of the pipe, the foot of the rod is one outside diameter above the outside bottom, so the invert is the shot elevation minus the outside diameter plus one wall. Under the pipe (bottom of pipe, BOP), the invert is one wall higher than the shot.

Example with the numbers already filled in: 105.20 + 4.36 = an HI of 109.56. A reading of 12.85 on top of 8" SDR 35 is an elevation of 96.71. Take off 0.68 ft (8.4" outside diameter less a 0.24" wall) and the invert is 96.03, right on the design invert. To lay that pipe to a 96.03 invert, the rod under the pipe should read 109.56 − (96.03 − 0.02) = 13.55.

Pipe sizes used

Outside diameters and walls come from the same tables the full app uses: PVC SDR 35 sewer pipe in 4" to 15" (outside diameters per ASTM D3034, wall = outside diameter ÷ 35) and PVC C900 DR18 in 4" to 12" (ductile iron outside diameters, wall = outside diameter ÷ 18). Those are minimum walls; real pipe runs a hair thicker, a few thousandths of a foot at most on these sizes. For any other pipe, put the rod on the invert or use your pipe's spec sheet.

Shoot a second known point before you trust an HI. If two hubs give two different HIs, find out why before laying pipe. The full app also handles architect's and Lenker rods and checks the HI against every hub you shoot.
