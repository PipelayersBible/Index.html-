How it works

A straight grade changes by the same amount every foot. Slope in percent is feet of change per 100 feet of run, so:

elevation at a distance = start invert ± (slope % ÷ 100) × distance

slope % = (second invert − first invert) ÷ distance × 100

inches per foot = slope % × 12 ÷ 100

Example: a 250 ft run of 8" sewer at 0.40% falling from 100.00 drops 1.00 ft, so the far end is 99.00. Every 25 ft station is 0.10 ft lower than the one before it. 0.40% is 0.048 inches per foot.

The rows go every interval from the start, plus one at the end of the run, the same way the full app lays out a grade sheet. Elevations round to the nearest 0.01 ft, with a half rounding away from zero, and feet-inches round to the nearest 1/16". Stations print the way they read on a cut sheet: 125 ft is 1+25.00.

Check every number against the plans, the cut sheet and your own instrument before you lay pipe. This page does straight grades only. The full app handles hubs, fittings, slope changes and rod readings for a whole run.

Slope in percent, inches per foot and fall per 100 feet

Plans for sewer and storm drain give slope in percent (or in feet per foot: 0.0040 is 0.40%). Plumbing work talks in inches per foot. They are the same thing written three ways:

To go the other way, percent = inches per foot ÷ 12 × 100. A quarter inch per foot is 0.25 ÷ 12 × 100 = 2.08%. The minimum slope for a line comes from the plans and the agency's standards, not from this table.
