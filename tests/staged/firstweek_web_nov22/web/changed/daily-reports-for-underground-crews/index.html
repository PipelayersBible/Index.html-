For foremen and the office

Daily reports for underground crews

The daily report fills in from the day's work on the crew's phones. The foreman checks it, signs it on the phone and sends it, before he leaves the job.

Filled in from the day's work

A lot of daily reports get typed up at night from memory. In The Pipelayer's Bible the report is already mostly done when the foreman opens it, because the crew logged the work on their phones as it happened:

  • Production. Footage from the day's runs is already there.
  • Delays. Delays logged on a run carry over with their times: utility conflict, inspector, weather, equipment down, material, survey/stakes, traffic control.
  • Extra work. Documented when it happens, not remembered later.
  • Crew and equipment. The day starts with the crew and equipment from the last report, so the foreman only changes what's different.
  • Field tickets. Snap a photo of the ticket and it goes with the report.

The foreman reads it over, adds anything that's missing, signs on the phone and sends it.

What the office sees

The office gets signed daily reports with production from every job and every crew. Each job shows percent complete by item, from what the crews log. Records can be searched, and runs, daily reports and permits can be exported. A crew's run progress shows as their phones send it, so the office can see how far they got without calling the foreman. A phone with no signal shows what it last sent.

811 tickets

Put the 811 ticket on the job and the foreman gets a warning when it's nearly expired: 7 days before, 2 days before, and on the day. That works in states where the ticket life is set by law. In the others the app shows no expiry date, so go by your ticket.

Works with no signal

The crew can keep logging with no signal. Records save on the phone and send when signal is back. Signing in and anything shared with the office need signal, and until a record has sent, the only copy is on that phone.
