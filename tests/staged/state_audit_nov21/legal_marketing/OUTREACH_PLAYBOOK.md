# Outreach playbook for out-of-state contractors (Nov 21, 2026; legal helper; not legal advice — check with the lawyer)

## Safe
- Your own posts; posts in groups (FB, LinkedIn, Reddit) where the group allows promotion.
- One-to-one DMs you type yourself. Replying to people who comment.
- Email — only once you have a postal address in the footer.
- Calls/texts only to people who reached out first (trial form, or a DM saying "text me").

## Never
- Text a number you found in a group, website or list. Texas (SB 140, Sept 2025), Washington (RCW 19.190.060, $500 a text), Maryland and Florida allow lawsuits per text.
- Bots, auto-DM tools, scraped or bought lists.
- Email without your address and an "unsubscribe / reply STOP" line; remove people within 10 days. No fake "Re:" or fake deadlines.
- Say "OSHA approved / compliant", "valid in all 50 states", "works everywhere", "legally binding signatures", "engineered".
- Post fake or AI-written reviews, or give free months for a good review. Post a customer quote only with their OK; add "(they got founder pricing)" when true.

## Sample first messages (change details to fit)
- DM after someone's post: "Saw your post about [cut sheets/dailies]. I'm an underground foreman out in SoCal and built a phone app for our pipe crews. Grade sheet from the stake, delay log, and the daily report fills itself from the day's work. Want to try it free for 30 days? No card. If not, no worries."
- Group post: "Foreman here. I built an app for sewer, storm and water crews because I was sick of retyping grade sheets and dailies. Works with no signal. Looking for a few crews outside California to try it free for 30 days and tell me what's wrong with it. pipelayersbible.com, or DM me."
- Email (after you have an address). Subject: "Pipe crew app, free 30-day trial". One short paragraph like the DM, then: "— Thomas Fontaine, The Pipelayer's Bible, [P.O. box], (909) 435-5250. Not interested? Reply STOP and I won't email again."
- One follow-up only: "Just bumping this once. If the timing's wrong, all good."

## Which states first
- Arizona and Nevada first: close, 811 rules confirmed from state law in the app, no state privacy or monitoring-notice law. (NV doesn't tax SaaS; AZ does after $100k of AZ sales.)
- Then Texas, Utah, Colorado, Oregon, Florida (811 confirmed from law). Texas: no texting at all.
- Hold off: Washington (text/email lawsuits, SaaS tax, own 4 ft trench rule); New York and Connecticut (monitoring-notice laws; NY ticket life unconfirmed); MA, NH, RI, SC, VT (811 only from the centers, not law).
- Re-check after the state audit finishes.

## What to say about state rules
"The app uses the job's state. For 811 ticket dates and trench depth it shows that state's rule where we've checked it against the state's own law. Where we haven't, it says so and doesn't guess. It doesn't replace your competent person, your safety program or your state's 811 law."
