## dig-ca-overview-1 | California 811: the dig law in short (USA North 811, DigAlert)
California's dig law is Government Code 4216 to 4216.24. Two centers answer 811: USA North 811 (north and central) and DigAlert (south). Mark the dig area in white first: the law says the excavator "shall delineate the area to be excavated" before calling. Call at least 2 working days and not more than 14 calendar days before digging. Working days are Monday to Friday, 7 AM to 5 PM, not holidays, and the call day does not count. Do not dig until every operator on the ticket has marked or answered through electronic positive response. A ticket is good for 28 days from issue; renew by the end of day 28. If it expires while work goes on, stop and get a new ticket. Tolerance zone: 24 inches on each side of the field mark. Inside it, find the line with hand tools before using power equipment. Hit a line: call the operator right away; call 911 if gas or hazardous liquid is escaping; tell the 811 center within 48 hours.

## dig-ca-tickets-1 | California 811: ticket life, renewal, no-response, high priority lines
California ticket rules (Gov. Code 4216.2, 4216.3). Notice: at least 2 working days, not more than 14 calendar days ahead. A ticket "shall be valid for 28 days from the date of issuance." If work goes past 28 days, renew online or by calling 811 "by the end of the 28th day." If the ticket expires while work is ongoing you must "immediately cease excavation, contact the regional notification center, and get a new ticket," and wait again for the operators. You may not start until all operators listed on the ticket have marked, given location information, or said they have no lines there. Every operator must post an electronic positive response before the legal start date and time. If one does not, tell the 811 center (4216.3(d)). Marks faded or gone: stop in that area and ask the center for remarks; the operator has 2 working days, not counting the request day. High priority lines (gas over 60 psig, petroleum, pressurized sewage, 60 kV or more electric, hazardous materials): if you will dig within 10 feet, the operator sets an onsite meeting and you may not dig until it is done. Farms and flood control can get an "area of continual excavation" ticket; that is not for pipeline construction crews.

## dig-az-overview-1 | Arizona 811: the dig law in short (Arizona 811)
Arizona's dig law is A.R.S. 40-360.21 to 40-360.32, enforced by the Arizona Corporation Commission. One center: Arizona 811. Mark the dig area with white lining before you call (40-360.22(C)); a person digging on a single-family home lot is excused. Operators must mark "as promptly as practicable, but not later than two working days." Working days skip Saturday, Sunday, legal holidays and the Friday after Thanksgiving. You may not start "before the location and marking are complete or the excavator is notified that marking is unnecessary." Marks are good for 15 working days from the date of marking. If work goes on, give notice at least 2 working days before they run out. Within 24 inches of a marked line, expose it carefully with hand tools and support it. Hit a line: tell the operator right away; gas or hazardous material, also call 911. Leave the hole open until the operator arrives. Fine: up to $5,000 per violation.

## dig-az-tickets-1 | Arizona 811: marks good 15 working days, renewals, large projects (2026 law)
Arizona ticket life: "The marking required by subsection B of this section is valid for fifteen working days from the date of the marking" (A.R.S. 40-360.22(J)). To keep digging past that, notify "at least two working days before the end of the validity period." The Corporation Commission's page says to call 811 "to have your marks refreshed." Whether the refresh gets a new ticket number is not confirmed. Landlords (apartment communities, mobile home parks) get up to 10 working days to mark their own lines. Operators answer through the interactive positive response system; check it before digging. If an operator can't finish marking in time it must tell you promptly and send someone to the site. A line is not abandoned until the operator tells you it is: "the excavator shall not treat the underground facility as abandoned until the excavator has received notification." New in 2026 (SB 1137, Laws 2026 ch. 147): for a large project, notify the one-call center "not less than 15 business days or 21 calendar days" before the start to set a coordination meeting, with a phased ticket plan agreed in the system. The effective date was not confirmed in the summary read.

## dig-nv-overview-1 | Nevada 811: the dig law in short (USA North 811)
Nevada's dig law is NRS 455.080 to 455.180 with rules in NAC 455. One center: USA North 811. Call at least 2 working days (7 AM to 5 PM, not weekends or holidays, call day not counted) and not more than 28 calendar days before digging. Mark the dig area in white first. Wait for every operator: the handbook says excavators "are required by law to wait until all operators" have given a positive response, even if the start time on the ticket has passed. A ticket is good for 28 calendar days from the date and time of the notification. It can be extended twice, 28 days each; after that the regulation requires a new notification (NAC 455.165). That is 84 days at most on one ticket. Hand tools only within 24 inches on either side of the outside of the line. Hit a line: notify the operator right away; in an emergency also 911.

## dig-nv-tickets-1 | Nevada 811: ticket types, two renewals, what to do after
Nevada ticket life (NAC 455.165): good "for 28 calendar days after the date and time that the excavator provided the notification." You may ask to extend by another 28 calendar days, and "An excavator may not request more than two extensions." If more time is needed after the original and both extensions, "the excavator shall submit a new notification" under NRS 455.110, which means a new ticket with the normal 2 working days' wait. Call it in at least 2 working days before the second renewal runs out so there is no gap. Renew by the end of day 28 and only if the marks are still clear. Ticket only the area you can reasonably dig within 14 calendar days (NAC 455.115). Ticket types in the USA North handbook: locate request; Renewal; Re-Mark (marks gone or unclear); No Response (an operator did not answer; you can resend every hour); Next Job Phase (copies the last ticket for the next section); Pothole ticket; Damage or Exposed notice; Emergency. A ticket should cover no more than half a mile. There is no project or long-term ticket. Designers should not open locate tickets for design; use the Project Design lookup to get operator contacts.

## dig-tx-overview-1 | Texas 811: the dig laws in short (Texas811)
Texas has two layers: Utilities Code chapter 251 (the one-call law for all lines) and Railroad Commission rule 16 TAC chapter 18 for pipelines (gas, hazardous liquid, CO2). Call 811 or use the Texas811 portal. Notice: at least 48 hours before digging, not counting weekends and legal holidays, and not more than 14 days ahead. Ticket life: 14 working days for pipeline locates unless you and the operator agree otherwise in writing. Not done: refresh the ticket for the area still to dig. Texas811 sends updates with a 2 business day notice. Tolerance zone for pipelines: half the pipe diameter plus at least 18 inches on each side. Wait for a positive response (marks, "all clear" or "no conflict"). None: give a second notice; if there is still none after 4 hours you may begin. Hit a pipeline: notify the operator through 811 within 1 hour, call 911 for a release, and file a damage report with the Railroad Commission within 30 days.

## dig-tx-tickets-1 | Texas 811: ticket life 14 working days, refresh, second notice
Texas pipeline locate tickets (16 TAC 18.1, 18.3, 18.4): a line locate ticket lasts 14 working days unless the excavator and operator agree to something else in writing. A working day is "Every day that is not a Saturday, a Sunday, or a legal holiday." When the work is not finished, "the excavator shall refresh the ticket by giving the notice," limited to the area still to be dug. Texas811 calls this an Update (or Update and Remark): it is "sent with a two-business day notice," and "If the most recent ticket is over 30 days old, a new ticket must be created." Whether an update prints a new ticket number is not confirmed. No positive response by the start time: you "shall not begin excavating until a second notice is given." If "there is no positive response within four hours, the excavator may begin excavating" (18.4(e)-(f)). Report the second call, and any operator that still did not answer, to the Railroad Commission through TDRF (18.11(d)). Protect the marks from the day you start until they are no longer needed (18.4(g)).

## dig-fl-overview-1 | Florida 811: the dig law in short (Sunshine 811)
Florida's dig law is chapter 556, the Underground Facility Damage Prevention and Safety Act. One center: Sunshine 811. Notice: "not less than 2 full business days before beginning any excavation" (10 full business days for underwater work). A ticket is valid for 30 calendar days. Not finished: give another notice for the part still to dig. Tolerance zone: 24 inches from the outer edge of either side of the marked line. Inside it, "use increased caution": hand dig, pothole, soft dig or vacuum. You must check the positive responses before you dig. Pre-mark in white when the site can't otherwise be identified. Hit a line: "immediately notify the member operator"; if gas or a hazardous substance escapes, call 911. Citations are $500 plus court costs, $2,500 for hazardous material pipelines.

## dig-fl-tickets-1 | Florida 811: ticket life 30 days, positive response, no-show rule
Florida ticket life (556.105(1)(c)): the information is valid for 30 calendar days. If the work is not finished, 556.114(2) says to "provide a subsequent notice to the system" for only the area still to be dug. Sunshine 811 says: "When you need to renew a standard ticket, do not include any areas that are finished." Operators must post a positive response within 2 full business days (10 for underwater). The excavator must verify the positive responses before digging (556.105(9)(c)). If an operator has not marked in the time allowed, the excavator may go ahead using reasonable care and detection equipment or other acceptable means to find the lines (556.105(6)(a)). If you can see there is a line there and it is not marked and has no positive response, contact the operator. No demolition until lines are marked or removed. High-priority lines (556.116): gas and hazardous liquid pipelines the operator calls critical. If you will dig within 15 feet of one and the operator told you so, give the operator your start date and time before digging. Design help: an operator answers a design request within 20 business days after its fee is paid (556.112).

## dig-wa-overview-1 | Washington 811: the dig law in short (Washington 811)
Washington's dig law is RCW 19.122, enforced by the Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC). Mark the boundary of the dig "with white lining or, when necessary, white pin flags," then call 811. Notice: "not less than two full business days and not more than 10 full business days" before the work-to-begin date. Since January 1, 2026 you "may not commence excavation until the excavator has received positive response" from every operator with lines in the area. Marks expire 45 calendar days after you gave notice; to dig after that, give a new notice. Marks are accurate to within 24 inches of the outside of the line on both sides. Find the exact location before digging there: hand digging when practical, vacuum or air tools. Hit a line: tell the operator and 811, call 911 if it is an emergency, don't bury it, and report the damage to the UTC within 45 days.

## dig-wa-tickets-1 | Washington 811: marks last 45 days, positive response, large projects
Washington ticket rules (RCW 19.122.030): notice 2 to 10 full business days before the work-to-begin date, unless you and the operators agree otherwise in writing. A business day is "any day other than Saturday, Sunday, or a legal local, state, or federal holiday." Markings "expire forty-five calendar days from the date that the excavator provided notice." For digging after that, "an excavator must provide additional notice to a one-number locator service." There is no renewal: it is a new ticket with the normal wait. The excavator must keep the marks accurate for 45 days or the length of the job, whichever is shorter. Positive response is required since January 1, 2026: no digging until every operator has answered. Large project means "a project that exceeds seven hundred linear feet." On large projects or multiple sites you must take reasonable steps to confer with the operators before each phase. Design locating is for planning only and can't be used to dig.

## dig-or-overview-1 | Oregon 811: the dig rules in short (Oregon 811)
Oregon's dig rules are ORS 757.542 to 757.562 and OAR chapter 952, written by the Oregon Utility Notification Center (Oregon 811). Pre-mark the dig area in white, then call at least 2 full business days and not more than 10 business days before digging. Do not start until you have "received a response from each operator of underground facilities in the area." Ticket life: the Center's 2026 Addendum says the rule was changed "from 45 days to 30 days," effective January 8, 2026. Older copies of the rule and the Center's own FAQ page still say 45 calendar days, so the current number is not confirmed. Until you check with Oregon 811, plan on 30 days. Past ticket life, call in a new locate request. Tolerance zone: 24 inches around the outside of the line on all sides. Use hand tools or other non-invasive methods there. Hit a line: tell the operator right away; gas or hazardous liquid release, call 911. Don't bury it without the operator's OK.

## dig-or-tickets-1 | Oregon 811: ticket life changed to 30 days in 2026 (was 45), no renewals
Oregon ticket life: the 2026 Addendum to the Oregon Standards Manual (Oregon Utility Notification Center, March 12, 2026) lists a change to OAR 952-001-0010(23) that shortens ticket life "from 45 days to 30 days," effective January 8, 2026. Copies of the rule dated through mid-2025 read "the 45 calendar day period after an excavator provides notice," and the Center's FAQ page still said 45 on Nov 20, 2026. The official current rule text was not confirmed in this pass, so treat 30 days as the safe number and ask Oregon 811. There is no renewal. The rule says not to keep digging after ticket life ends "unless you re-notify the Center" (0090): that is a new locate request with the normal 2 full business days' notice. Do not start more than 10 business days after the notice without re-notifying. Keep the marks for the life of the job or of the ticket, whichever is shorter. Marks gone: stop and ask the Center for a remark. There is no legal "priority" locate. Less than 2 business days' notice is only for emergencies (0060).

## dig-co-overview-1 | Colorado 811: the dig law in short (Colorado 811)
Colorado's dig law is C.R.S. 9-1.5-101 to 9-1.5-108, with the Underground Damage Prevention Safety Commission hearing violations. One center: Colorado 811. Notice: "at least two (2) full business days prior to any excavation," not counting the call day. Marks are good for 30 days from the ticket's due date, or until you can't see them, whichever is first. To go on, notify Colorado 811 at least 2 business days before the 30 days end; once it has run out you need a new ticket. Tolerance zone: the width of the line plus 18 inches on each side. Use non-destructive digging there (hand or vacuum). Check that every member on the ticket has posted a positive response before you dig. If one has not by the locate-by date, send an Excavator Re-Notification first. Hit a line: stop, notify 811 and the operator right away; gas release, clear the area and call 911.

## dig-co-tickets-1 | Colorado 811: ticket types, 30-day marks, updates
Colorado ticket types (Colorado 811 handbook pp. 6-13): Normal (2 full business days ahead); Multiple Day (encouraged for jobs over 1,000 linear feet in populated areas); Emergency; Damage; Non-Compliance (you are asking for a response in less than 2 full business days); Excavator Re-Notification (a member did not answer or the locate is incomplete); Relocate/Refresh (marks no longer visible); Meet; Cancel; Engineering and Subsurface Utility Engineering (design only, never for digging; owners answer within 10 business days). Life: "Locate marks are only valid for thirty (30) days from the ticket's due date" or until no longer visible. The excavator "shall notify Colorado 811 at least two (2) business days" before that ends, and "A new ticket must be processed after this time has expired." An Update Lite can be used on a ticket processed in the last 30 calendar days when nothing on it changes. Emergency tickets: members answer within 1 hour in business hours, 2 hours after hours. Meets: if a member is 15 minutes late with no contact, send a Re-Notification. Whether an update gets a new ticket number is not confirmed.

## dig-ut-overview-1 | Utah 811: the dig law in short (Blue Stakes of Utah 811)
Utah's dig law is Utah Code Title 54, Chapter 8a. One center: Blue Stakes of Utah 811. Notice: not "less than 48 hours before excavation begins" and not "more than 14 days before." The 48 hours count business days only; business hours are 8 AM to 4 PM, Monday to Friday, not holidays. A notice is "valid for 21 days from the day on which the notice is given." To keep going, renew at least 48 hours and no more than 7 calendar days before it expires. Blue Stakes gives an update a different ticket number. Tolerance zone: 24 inches on either side of the outside edge of the mark (or half the marked diameter plus 24 inches from the center). No power equipment inside the zone until you have found the exact location with hand tools. Operator did not mark or answer in 48 hours: file a no response notice; the operator then has 4 business hours. Hit a line: tell the operator right away; gas, electric or danger to life, call 911. No backfill until the operator says so.

## dig-ut-tickets-1 | Utah 811: ticket life 21 days, updates, no-response notice
Utah ticket life (54-8a-4(6)): a notice is "valid for 21 days from the day on which the notice is given," and marks are good for no more than 21 calendar days (54-8a-5(3)). If digging will go past that, give a renewal notice at least 48 hours but no more than 7 calendar days before the ticket expires. Blue Stakes ticket types: New, Update (UPDT), No Response Notice (NRSP), Re-Mark (RMRK), Retransmit (RXMT), Cancel (CNCL). Its ticket field guide says the old ticket number is the "Same number if a New ticket; different if Updated," and each ticket prints an "Update By" date and time. Send the update by that time. Operators answer through Blue Stakes' electronic positive response system. If an operator has not marked or answered within 48 hours, you may not dig where you know or can see there is an unmarked line until you have filed a no response notice and the operator has arranged to mark. The operator must mark "within four business hours of the association receiving a no response notice." Very large jobs: an operator that can't mark an extensive excavation in time may "request a meeting at the proposed excavation site." Colors follow the Common Ground Alliance Uniform Color Code.

## dig-ga-overview-1 | Georgia 811: the dig law in short (Georgia 811)
Georgia's dig law is the Georgia Underground Facility Protection Act, O.C.G.A. 25-9, enforced by the Public Service Commission. One center: Georgia 811. Notice: the notice period starts at 7 AM on the first business day after Georgia 811 gets your request and runs at least 2 business days, so a Monday request is good to dig Thursday at 7 AM. A request after 4:30 PM counts as the next business day. You may start early only if every owner has already answered clear or marked. Check the Positive Response Information System (PRIS) before digging. A locate request is good for 30 calendar days. Georgia 811 allows a normal ticket to be updated two times (90 days); each update is a new ticket number. Tolerance zone: the width of the line plus 18 inches on either side. Hit a line: stop, notify Georgia 811 and the owner; gas or hazardous liquid, call 911. Fines up to $10,000 per violation.

## dig-ga-tickets-1 | Georgia 811: ticket life 30 days, two updates, large projects, PRIS
Georgia ticket life (25-9-6(c)): digging may not take place more than 30 calendar days after the ticket's effective date; to keep going, "an additional locate request with appropriate notice must be submitted." The added request can't make the area bigger. Georgia 811 calls this an Update: "An Update extends the life of an existing Normal ticket. A new ticket will be generated." It can only be sent between the "Updateable On" and "Update By" dates on the ticket, and "a Normal Ticket may be updated a maximum of two times" (90 days in all). A third update needs a reason and is reported to the PSC. Jobs over 1 linear mile or over 90 days: call 811 and ask for the Large Project team. PRIS: you are "responsible for being aware of all information timely entered into the PRIS" before you dig. If an owner has not answered, Georgia 811 sends a second request; if there is still no answer by noon that business day you "may proceed" as long as "there is no visible and obvious evidence of the presence of an unmarked underground facility" (25-9-7(e)). Don't ask for re-marks over and over; an owner can bill up to $100 for one. Design locate request: owners answer within ten business days; it "shall not be used for excavation purposes." Emergency tickets: no digging after 7 AM on the third business day after the request.

## dig-nc-overview-1 | North Carolina 811: the dig law in short (NC811)
North Carolina's dig law is the Underground Utility Safety and Damage Prevention Act, G.S. Chapter 87, Article 8A (amended in 2025). One center: NC811. Notice: at least 3 full working days before the start date (10 to 20 for work near underwater lines). Working days are every day but Saturday, Sunday and State holidays. A notice expires 28 calendar days after the work start date. To keep digging, give a new notice the same way; NC811 calls it an update and it gets a new ticket number. Check positive response before you dig. Tolerance zone: 24 inches on each side of the outside edge of the mark, or half the diameter plus 24 inches from the centerline when the size is known. No mechanized equipment in the zone until you can see the line. Hit a line: notify NC811 and the operator right away; gas, electricity or danger, call 911 first. No backfill until cleared.

## dig-nc-tickets-1 | North Carolina 811: ticket life 28 days, updates, 3-hour notice, size limits
North Carolina ticket life (87-122(a)): the notice expires 28 calendar days after the work start date, and "No excavation or demolition may continue after this 28-day period unless" a new notice is given in the same manner as the first. NC811's ticket system treats that as an Update, which "Generates a new ticket number linked to the old one" and must be processed before the "Update By" date (from the ticket software vendor's help page; NC811's own pages would not open). Size: a notice may cover one parcel, or the lesser of five adjoining parcels or a quarter mile, and only what can be finished in 28 days. Positive response (87-122(c)): confirm through the system that every operator has answered before you dig. If one has not, you may go ahead only if there are no visible signs of a line (pole, marker, pedestal, meter, valve). If there are signs, send an additional notice; the operator must mark "within three hours from the time the additional notice is received." You may start before the 3 days are up only when all operators have already answered. Design notice (87-127): operators answer within 10 working days; it "may not be used for excavation purposes." Underwater (subaqueous) lines: 10 to 20 full working days' notice.

## dig-ny-overview-1 | New York 811: the dig rules in short (UDig NY, New York 811)

Emergency: "a condition that poses a clear and immediate danger to life or health" or a major loss of property. Scheduling problems and customer demands are not emergencies. Operators try to reach you within one hour and are on site within three when needed. No ticket needed: farm tilling or planting shallower than 18 inches; gardening shallower than 12 inches. Homeowners and farmers must call for anything else.

## dig-sc-overview-1 | South Carolina 811: the dig law in short (SC811)
South Carolina's dig law is the Underground Facility Damage Prevention Act, S.C. Code Title 58, Chapter 36. One center: SC811. Wait: operators "have three full working days to mark," not counting the day you call. A normal request is good for 15 working days from its start time. Send an Extension any time before it runs out; an Extension does not refresh the paint. Marks gone: send a Remark, which makes a new ticket with a new number and gives operators three full working days. Once a request has expired you need a new one. Pre-mark in white paint, flags or stakes when the site can't be clearly identified. Before digging, verify that every operator has answered in Positive Response. No answer and no sign of a line: you may proceed. Signs of a line: request a No Show; the operator has three hours. Tolerance zone: 24 inches on either side of the mark plus half the diameter if known. No machines there until you can see the line; machines may remove pavement only. Hit a line: notify SC811 and the operator right away; danger or a release, call 911 first.

## dig-sc-tickets-1 | South Carolina 811: ticket types, large projects, emergencies, complaints
South Carolina ticket types (SC811 Excavator Manual 2026): Normal; Design (operators have 15 full working days; no digging on it); Emergency; Large Project; Subaqueous (underwater: 10 working days' notice, tolerance zone 15 feet either side). Large Project: a job expected to take more than 90 days, or that meets the highway, development or utility size tests. Notify the center at least 30 days before starting; a planning meeting is held within 15 days; each phase gets its own start and end dates and needs no updates while work stays inside them. Emergency: "any sudden or unforeseen event that requires immediate action." Notify SC811 and the operators as soon as practicable; operators make contact within three hours. Calling it an emergency does not remove liability for damage. Bad weather: ice or snow can be an extraordinary circumstance that lets an operator post Code 110 with a later marking date. Complaints: file with the Attorney General's Office within 45 days of the incident. Fine amounts are not in the manual and were not confirmed.

## dig-ok-overview-1 | Oklahoma 811: the dig law in short (OKIE811)
Oklahoma's dig law is the Underground Facilities Damage Prevention Act, 63 O.S. 142.1 to 142.13 (last amended November 1, 2024). The Corporation Commission enforces it for oil and gas pipelines. One center: OKIE811. Notice: at least 48 hours before digging, not counting the day you call, Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays. Demolition: 7 business days. "Notice shall expire fourteen (14) calendar days from the excavation start date." To keep going, update the ticket (Extend, or Re-Mark if marks are gone) at least 4 business days before it expires; operators get 48 hours to respond. A ticket covers no more than 500 linear feet in a city or town, or one linear mile outside. White line the dig area before you call. Check positive responses before starting. Operator missing: send a Second Notice. Approximate location: a strip two feet on either side of the line. Only hand digging there until the line is exposed; machines may remove pavement only. Hit a line: notify the operator and OKIE811 immediately; gas escaping, leave and call 911. Hold the backfill until repairs are done.

## dig-ok-tickets-1 | Oklahoma 811: ticket types, large projects, emergencies, exemptions
Oklahoma ticket types (OKIE811 Excavator Guide 2026 pp. 16-19): Normal; Emergency; Demolition (7 business days, after permits); Design Survey (operators have 14 calendar days; "No excavation may take place on a design or survey notice"); Pre-Excavation Meeting request for large projects (file 14 or more days ahead); Re-Mark / Extend, both add 14 days; Second Notice when an operator missed its response or the locate is incomplete. Large project: over the ticket size limits or expected to last more than 90 days. "Watch and protect": if an operator has posted that response, do not dig within ten feet of its marks without its person there (63 O.S. 142.6). Emergency: an "unavoidable situation that poses a danger to life, health, or property." Digging "may begin immediately, providing reasonable precautions are taken," and operators must be told promptly. A false emergency can make you pay the response costs. Exempt: farm cultivation; a person digging on their own property, unless in an easement or right-of-way or working for hire. A repeat willful violator can be barred by court order from excavating. Fine amounts are not in the guide and were not confirmed.

## dig-ia-overview-1 | Iowa 811: the dig law in short (Iowa One Call)
Iowa's dig law is Iowa Code chapter 480; the Attorney General enforces it. One center: Iowa One Call. Notice: "at least forty-eight hours prior to the commencement" of digging, not counting the notice day, weekends and legal holidays. A ticket is "valid for twenty-five (25) calendar days beginning on the day the forty-eight-hour period concludes." There is no renewal: for a longer job, request a new locate ticket before the old one runs out. You can't dig on another excavator's ticket. Use white paint, flags or stakes to show the dig area unless it is clearly defined or you used electronic white-lining. Operators must post a positive response within the 48 hours. Check the Electronic Positive Response System before starting; if one is missing, contact the operator or Iowa One Call. Tolerance zone: 18 inches on either side of the line. Hand-dig test holes there; no machinery other than accepted methods such as vacuum excavation. Hit a line: tell the operator as soon as practical and do not backfill until it has looked; gas or liquid escaping, call 911.

## dig-ia-tickets-1 | Iowa 811: ticket types, fines, farm exemption
Iowa ticket types (Iowa One Call manual pp. 15-19): Regular locate; Emergency locate; Dig-In notice (reports damage or contact; it does not create a locate ticket); Joint meet (a preconstruction meeting for complex sites, which can stand in for white-lining); Design information request (maps for designers); Design locate request (available after a five working day wait). Large sites can be planned on one Parent Ticket that the system splits into smaller tickets. Emergency: "a condition where there is clear and immediate danger to life or health" or loss of essential service. You may start at once with reasonable care and must notify the center as soon as practical. Falsifying an emergency can be penalized. Civil penalties (Iowa Code 480.6): gas or hazardous liquid pipelines, up to $10,000 per violation per day, capped at $500,000; other lines, up to $1,000 per day, capped at $20,000. Normal farming is not excavation, but ripping deeper than 15 inches is not "normal farming." Homeowners are not exempt. Operators do not locate for depth.

## dig-la-overview-1 | Louisiana 811: the dig law in short (Louisiana 811)
Louisiana's dig law is the Underground Utilities and Facilities Damage Prevention Law, R.S. 40:1749.11 to 1749.27 (amended effective August 1, 2026). One center: Louisiana 811. Notice (R.S. 40:1749.13): at least 48 hours and not more than 120 hours ahead, not counting weekends and holidays; the wait runs from 7:00 a.m. on the next working day after you call. Marks are good "as long as visible, but not longer than twenty calendar days, including weekends and holidays," from the mark-by time. Marks gone or work not done in time: give another notice to the center. Forestry, farm and marine digging: up to 30 calendar days. A notice designated a large project with a written agreement: 90 calendar days. Operators give a positive response through the center by the mark-by time; bad weather extends it. The marked location is the width of the line "plus eighteen inches on either side." Marks use the APWA color code. An excavator who called and followed the law is immune from civil liability to an operator that did not join the center or did not mark, unless the excavator was negligent. Fine amounts and the damage steps were not confirmed in this pass.

## dig-ms-overview-1 | Mississippi 811: the dig law in short (Mississippi 811)
Mississippi's dig law is Miss. Code 77-13-1 and after, amended by HB 1191 effective July 1, 2025. One center: Mississippi 811 (MS811). Ticket life went "from 14 to 20 calendar days" for Normal, Emergency, Impending Emergency, Short Notice and Design tickets. Update at least 3 working days before a ticket expires so there is no gap. (MS811's older portal guide says an update adds 14 calendar days; whether that is now 20 is not confirmed.) The wait before digging was not on the page read and is not confirmed here: go by the dig date on your ticket. Tolerance zone: 18 inches plus one-half of the diameter of the line when the diameter is shown at the marks, measured from the outside edge of the marks. Use non-invasive methods there (hand digging, vacuum), support the line, and keep machines a safe distance away. Emergency tickets: operators respond within 2 hours, and you must name a contact who will be on site. Large projects (can't be finished in 20 calendar days): optional preconstruction meeting and a signed agreement before work.

## dig-sd-overview-1 | South Dakota 811: the dig law in short (South Dakota 811)
South Dakota's dig law is SDCL chapter 49-7A, with the One-Call Notification Board writing the rules. One center: South Dakota 811. Notice (49-7A-5, 2026 Senate Bill 9): "at least forty-eight hours prior to the commencement of the excavation." The 48 hours start at 12:01 a.m. the day after you send the notice and skip Saturdays, Sundays and state legal holidays. Operators mark within "eighteen inches horizontally from the exterior sides of the underground facilities." Keep "a minimum horizontal clearance of eighteen inches" between the marked line and the cutting edge of any machine. If you must dig closer than 18 inches, expose the line first with hand tools or an approved noninvasive method, then protect and support it before using the machine again. How long a ticket lasts, the damage steps and the fine amounts were not in the text read and are not confirmed here: go by the ticket and South Dakota 811.

## dig-wy-overview-1 | Wyoming 811: the dig law in short (One-Call of Wyoming)
Wyoming's dig law is the Wyoming Underground Facilities Notification Act, Wyo. Stat. 37-12-301 to 37-12-307. One center: One-Call of Wyoming (811). Notice: "at least two (2) full business days, but not more than fourteen (14) business days prior to any excavation." A job that runs longer than 14 business days needs the notice given again for each following 14 business day period. Mark the dig area before the locator comes, unless one operator can find the site from the address, there was a site meeting, or it is an emergency. Operators mark "within twenty-four (24) inches horizontally" of the outside of the line, in APWA colors, within two full business days. Reasonable care is always required, and it calls for hand or soft digging where needed to protect the line. Hit or touch a line: notify the operator and the center immediately. Gas or hazardous liquid released: call 911 first. Do not hide damage or repair it without authorization. In a dispute the excavator has to prove reasonable care. If the operator missed its deadline or marked wrong, an excavator who used reasonable care is not liable. Penalty amounts were not confirmed.

## dig-hi-overview-1 | Hawaii 811: the dig law in short (Hawaii One Call Center)
Hawaii's dig law is HRS chapter 269E, under the Public Utilities Commission. One center: the Hawaii One Call Center (811). Notice: "at least five working days but not more than twenty-eight calendar days prior to commencing excavation," except in an emergency. ## dig-nd-overview-1 | North Dakota 811: the dig law in short (North Dakota One Call)
North Dakota's dig law is N.D.C.C. chapter 49-23. One center: North Dakota One Call (811). Notice: the handbook says "at least 48 hours (excluding weekends and holidays)" before digging. A ticket is good for 21 calendar days from the start time on it. The law says an excavator "may not use a location more than twenty-one days" after the planned dig date without arrangements with the operators; if no digging happened in the first 21 days, ask for a relocate first. After expiry, get a new ticket. There is no blanket ticket. One request covers at most three city blocks in town, or four quarter sections or five linear miles in the country. Show the site with white marking, digital white lining or project staking. Operators mark a strip two feet on either side of the line. Dig by hand within 24 inches of a located line. Knowing of damage and not reporting it, or backfilling over it, is a class A misdemeanor. Civil penalty up to $25,000. Farm work shallower than 18 inches and gardening shallower than 12 inches are not excavation.

## dig-wi-overview-1 | Wisconsin 811: the dig law in short (Diggers Hotline)
Wisconsin's dig law is Wis. Stat. 182.0175. One center: Diggers Hotline (811). A Public Service Commission panel hears complaints. Notice: "not less than 3 working days before the start of nonemergency excavation." Working days are days other than Saturday, Sunday and legal holidays. There is no set ticket life. Give a new notice if the dig does not start "within 10 days of the scheduled start date," if marks are destroyed or covered, or if work stops for more than 10 days. Keep "an estimated minimum clearance of 18 inches" between a marked, unexposed line and the cutting edge of the machine. Once the line is exposed the clearance can drop to twice the machine's limit of control or 12 inches, whichever is greater. Owners mark within 3 working days in the ANSI uniform colors. Before backfilling, inspect any line you exposed. If a line may be damaged, notify the owner immediately and do not backfill until it has been inspected. Gas or liquid that may endanger life: call 911. Emergency: take all reasonable precautions and notify owners as promptly as possible. Fine amounts were not confirmed.
