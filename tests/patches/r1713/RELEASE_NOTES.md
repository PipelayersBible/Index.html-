# r17.13 · Nov 20, 2026

## 811 rules fixed for 10 states
The state rules now match the law or the 811 center's current rules:
- **North Carolina:** tolerance zone is 24 inches each side of the mark for every line (it said only transmission lines).
- **Illinois:** tolerance zone 18 inches; check positive response before you dig (since Jan 1, 2026); pre-mark in black on snow. Ticket life stays 25 working days.
- **Virginia:** wait is 48 hours from 7 AM the next working day (a Monday call is good Thursday).
- **Pennsylvania:** call 3 to 10 business days ahead. The law sets no ticket life; call again if you leave the site for more than 2 business days.
- **Louisiana:** tolerance zone 18 inches each side.
- **Oklahoma:** tolerance zone 2 feet each side; extend or re-mark at least 4 business days before expiry.
- **Minnesota:** white marks always required. **Florida:** white marks when the site can't be described; emergency line added.
- **Missouri:** unmarked or wrong marks: the owner has 2 hours. **Kentucky:** send a second notice; hand dig inside the tolerance zone.
- Georgia and North Dakota not changed yet (waiting on a phone call / a current handbook).

## Report a serious injury
A short line tells the crew who the company reports to and how fast, by the job's state (then the company's state):
federal OSHA 8 hours for a death, 24 hours for a hospital stay, amputation or lost eye; stricter in California,
Washington, Utah, Alaska and Hawaii. State not known: report within 8 hours to be safe. It shows on the state Rules
screen, on a new safety event in the Job Book, and on the confined space emergency panel.

## Safety wording
- **Heat Illness talk:** Oregon and Maryland go by the heat index, Washington and California by the air temperature;
  Oregon's written rest-break schedule; Nevada's heat plan.
- **Washington walkways:** guardrails from 4 ft (talks and the Washington rules).
- **Trench Sloping on a Washington job:** the short-term Type A slope (0.5:1) is not offered; a line says to use Type A
  0.75:1. Your competent person decides on site.
- **Cave-in depth notes follow the job's state** first, then where the phone is. If neither is known, the app uses the
  strictest rule (Washington, 4 ft).
